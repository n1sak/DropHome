# Concept

## The problem with folders

A folder tree asks you to do two hard things. When you save a file you have to invent a category for it, and months later you have to guess which category your past self invented. Most people give up on both, which is why a typical drive is one giant Downloads folder and a search box.

People are not bad at organizing, though. They are good at it in physical space. You do not search for your toothbrush. You know the fridge is where the grocery list goes and that the box of old school notebooks is somewhere in the attic. Spatial memory is strong, it is shared across people, and it costs nothing to learn. Memory champions use exactly this trick and call it a memory palace.

Roomy takes that seriously. The top level of your storage is a house. Rooms are the parts of your life. Furniture is what a folder wishes it were: a place with a meaning.

## One grammar, repeated in every room

The piece that makes this more than a skin on folders is that furniture carries semantics, and the same semantics repeat everywhere.

| Furniture | Role | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Desk, workbench, coffee table, recipe box | `active` | What you are working on now |
| Closet, wardrobe, shelves, trunk | `archive` | Finished, kept, grouped by year |
| Corkboard, fridge door, photo wall, pegboard, mirror | `display` | Pinned where you can see it |
| Bathtub, planter | `drafts` | Rough ideas still soaking |
| Bookshelf, pantry, toolbox | `reference` | Kept handy to look things up |
| Memory chest | `memories` | Keepsakes |
| Safe, filing cabinet | `vault` | Important documents |
| Record crate, projector | `media` | Music and video |
| Backup rack | `backup` | Device backups and exports |
| Hamper | `cleanup` | Messy files waiting for a tidy-up |
| Junk drawer | `misc` | The honest last resort |
| Porch, mailbox | `inbox` | Delivered, not unpacked |
| Bins | `trash` | Thrown out, rescued for 30 days |
| Console table, guest book, TV | `recent`, `shared`, `screen` | Live views. Nothing is stored in them |

Once you know that a surface means "now" and a closet means "the past", you can read a room you have never seen. It is also what lets the sorter work in a house the owner designed: it never aims at "the study desk", it aims at "an active surface in a room about school", and the house resolves that to real furniture.

## The standard house

Everyone starts with the same floor plan, three columns wide, so the layout itself becomes familiar.

```
                /  attic  \              cold storage: old work, old photos
   +-----------+-----------+-----------+
   |  bedroom  | bathroom  |   study   |  private things | works in progress | school
   +-----------+-----------+-----------+
   |  kitchen  |   hall    |  living   |  food, household | the hub | photos and memories
   +-----------+-----------+-----------+
   | workshop  |    den    |  cellar   |  code, projects | music, video | backups
   +-----------+-----------+-----------+
 mailbox  porch                    bins
```

Upstairs is for the mind, the ground floor is for living, and below ground is for making and keeping. Things arrive on the left of the house (mailbox, porch) and leave on the right (bins). The hall stores nothing. It is the hub: what you touched last, and everything you have shared.

## Time is part of the space

A real house shows you time without being asked. Roomy does the same with three small signals. Files gather visible dust the longer they go untouched, and after two years they grow a cobweb. A room's lights are on only if you have used it in the last two weeks, so one look at the house at night tells you where your life has been lately. And spring cleaning notices what has been sitting out for more than a year and offers to box it up and carry it to the attic, where old things belong.

## How it maps to the challenge

**Reimagine how we organize digital content.** A spatial file system with a learnable grammar, not a folder tree with pictures on it.

**Turn digital chaos into something useful.** The porch is the front door for chaos. Drop a messy pile and the house unpacks it, with a plain reason for every placement, and an undo.

**Turn files into action.** Pinning is an action with a visible result: the list appears on the fridge, the photo plays on the TV. Spring cleaning turns neglect into a suggested move.

**Make photo libraries smarter.** The living room is a photo library with a point of view: a coffee table for what is new, albums by year, a wall for favourites, a chest for childhood, and a TV that plays this month.

**The Dropbox basics still work.** Cloud storage, live sync between windows, share links, and a drop link that lets other people put files in your mailbox.

## What comes next

Flipbook animations for the furniture, and more pieces to furnish with. Vision for photos, so the sorter can tell a receipt from a sunset. Shared rooms, where a roommate or a project team can walk into one room of your house. A guest view of a single room as the share page.
